The shift

From ad buyer to growth owner

Most performance teams optimize for ROAS. I've spent a decade learning why that's the wrong question — and building systems that ask the right one.

"Most accounts leak 40–60% of budget before we even touch bids. Fix the measurement. Then fix the campaigns."

I started in paid search in 2009, landed my first client in Q4 2011, and spent the next decade running acquisition across e-commerce, healthcare, B2B, and home services — including time as a Google Account Strategist where I saw firsthand how the platform's AI actually makes decisions.

The turning point: rebuilding attribution for a healthcare client and discovering their "winning" campaigns were masking a CPA that was 3× what they thought. Bad data produces confident wrong decisions. I now treat measurement as the first deliverable, not an afterthought.

Inside a team, that means I own the full acquisition function — tracking infrastructure, unit economics modeling, channel strategy, creative testing, and reporting that connects ad spend to real business outcomes. Not just the dashboard, but the decisions the dashboard drives.

How I work

The operating sequence

01
Measurement audit before anything else

GA4 event architecture, GTM triggers, Meta CAPI, CRM match-back. If the data is wrong, every decision that follows is wrong. This comes first, always.

02
Build the unit economics model

CAC by channel, LTV by cohort and product line, target payback period aligned to business cash position. ROAS is a proxy — this model is the north star.

03
Structured tests with clear hypotheses

Creative, audience, landing page, and offer experiments — one variable at a time, with enough statistical weight to produce a real signal before scaling the winner.

04
Scale when the math says scale

Not when the calendar says Q4. Budget increases when CAC is below target, LTV:CAC is healthy, and payback is within the agreed window.

05
Reporting that drives decisions

Looker Studio dashboards for CMOs and CFOs — blended CAC, channel MER, cohort payback curves. Built to surface the decision, not to show effort.
